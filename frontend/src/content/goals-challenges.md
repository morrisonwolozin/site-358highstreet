## Secondary Goals:

- Net-zero utility costs through on- and off-site solar generation.
- Low heating and cooling demand through a high-performance thermal envelope.
- Strong sound and fire separation between apartments.
- Improved livability — half baths, in-apartment laundry, mudrooms, and conditioned storage added to each apartment.
- Elimination of bulk water intrusion through foundation waterproofing and site drainage improvements.
- Continuous indoor air quality monitoring via networked sensors.

## Retrofit Scope of Work:

### Envelope & Thermal Performance

- Deep energy retrofit of above-grade walls and roof — continuous exterior insulation, new sheathing, and rebuilt overhangs.
- Closed-cell spray foam insulation in roof cavities and crawlspace walls.
- Replacement of all windows with triple-pane apartments, reframed to align with exterior insulation plane.
- New crawlspace floor assembly — dimple mat moisture barrier, rigid insulation, and Advantech subfloor.

### Foundation & Site

- Perimeter excavation, foundation waterproofing, and replacement of footing drains.
- Roof drainage added to foundation drain system.
- Site grading to improve surface water diversion and expand parking.

### Additions

- North addition providing a new entry, laundry alcove, and half bath for each apartment.
- West addition providing conditioned and unconditioned storage for each apartment, with separate crawlspace access.

### Separation Walls

- Replacement of original above-grade party wall with insulated double-stud construction for sound and fire separation, estimated STC > 65.
- New insulated party wall between crawlspaces for sound, fire and air separation.

### Mechanical & Ventilation

- Ducted cold-climate heat pump heating and cooling for each apartment, with air handler located in its apartment crawlspace.
- Heat pump domestic water heater in each apartment's storage room.
- Energy recovery ventilator (ERV) for each apartment providing continuous balanced ventilation.
- Variable-speed kitchen range exhaust with remote exterior fan.

### Electrical & Lighting

- New LED ceiling lighting throughout, including closets — switched receptacles eliminated.
- On-site solar photovoltaic generation — 8.36 kW rooftop array serving apartment 2.

### Interior

- New kitchens with full appliance packages.
- New bathrooms and fixtures throughout.

### Site Amenities

- Raised garden beds.

## Pre-construction Conditions

### Crawlspace Flooding

Poor site drainage and unmanaged roof runoff caused the crawlspace to flood after major rain events. A December 2023 storm left an inch of standing water that took over 24 hours to drain, with residual puddles persisting for days. The chronic moisture elevated indoor humidity roughly 20% above outdoor levels — acceptable in winter but excessive in summer and shoulder seasons, and a long-term mold risk.

### Aging Oil-Fired Boiler

The boiler was functional but at end of life. Its crawlspace location made maintenance difficult. It required an induced draft fan whose discharge was insufficiently elevated above grade a code violation which was flagged in the pre-sale home inspection. Its operational noise was excessive. Its fuel oil tank was located in the crawlspace; an undetected leak in combination with water intrusion and drainage through the floor drain would have contaminated the site.

### Unequal Electric Service Allocation

With two tenant meters and no landlord meter, shared loads — the electric clothes dryer, boiler plant, and washing machine — were assigned arbitrarily to one apartment's service or the other.

### Insufficient Interior Lighting

Bedrooms and living rooms had no built-in lighting. The kitchen had one overhead fixture. General lighting depended on switched receptacles requiring plug-in lamps throughout.

### No Exterior Lighting

The site had no exterior lighting of any kind.

### Poor Indoor Air Quality - Insufficient Ventilation

Air leakage testing measured 3.68 ACH50 — approximately one natural air change every five hours — confirming that infiltration alone was inadequate for acceptable ventilation. AirThings monitors confirmed poor air quality in both apartments.

### No Crawlspace Fire or Sound Separation

The original crawlspace was open between apartments, providing no barrier to fire spread, sound transmission, or air movement.

### Excessive Inter-Apartment Air and Sound Transfer

Air moved freely between apartments. CO₂ readings in the unoccupied Apartment 1 matched those in occupied Apartment 2, and painting odors from Apartment 1 were detectable in Apartment 2.## Other Deficiencies
Each apartment had one bathroom. Apartment 2 lacked a dishwasher. Apartment 1's refrigerator and range burners had failed.
